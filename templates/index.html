How tracking IDs work, and where they can fail

The detector finds objects in each frame. The tracker then matches today's boxes to the boxes it saw a moment ago and reuses the same ID number. This is a best-effort match, not a guarantee.

  • An object hidden behind something for long enough can come back with a new ID.
  • People or cars that cross paths or overlap can swap IDs.
  • Fast motion, motion blur, low light and small or distant objects make detection flicker, which breaks tracks.
  • Cuts and camera jumps usually reset tracking.
  • So "unique IDs" in the summary can be higher than the number of real objects.
